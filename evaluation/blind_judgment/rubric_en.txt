NARRATIVE EVALUATION — PROTOCOL V1 (600 texts, English)
For every ID, read the prompt and the whole continuation. Do not consult models, reference answers, other people's judgments or previous results. Do not guess where the text comes from. Rate every text once only and record the concrete reason for the judgment.

Grammar (1-10): 9-10 fluent and grammatically correct text; 7-8 minor errors that do not hinder understanding; 6 understandable text despite localized errors; 4-5 recurring errors that hinder understanding; 1-3 text largely incomprehensible.
Consistency (1-10): 9-10 identities, attributes, objects and sequence of events stable; 7-8 marginal ambiguities or small imprecise details; 6 interpretable narrative core without substantial contradictions; 4-5 unresolved contradictions about identities, properties, attributes or events that change the meaning of a scene; 1-3 central identities/events contradictory or uninterpretable.
Plot (1-10): 9-10 clear and well-connected development; 7-8 simple but coherent; 6 minimal understandable plot with connected events; 4-5 disconnected or repetitive sequences, or essential causality missing; 1-3 no understandable plot.
Category S: all three >=6 and a coherent narrative core. P: recognizable core but at least one substantial problem (at least one score <=5). N: severe loss of coherence or understandability (at least one score <=3).
Do not fail a text only for a negative ending, personification, talking animals, magic, a simple plot or an ending truncated by the generation limit. An explicitly motivated transformation is allowed; an unmotivated change of identity or property is an error. Do not require a moral. A new character is allowed if it does not ambiguously replace the protagonist. Do not automatically penalize a narrative coincidence if the events remain interpretable. Penalize an objective contradiction of the prompt, regardless of grammar.
Anchor examples: "Mia found a red ball. She gave it to Tom. Tom played with it." S; "Mia gave the only ball to Tom, then said she had never given it away" P; "Mia was a cat. Without explanation Mia was a boy, and Tom was Mia" N if the central identity becomes incomprehensible. A talking bear or a ball that explicitly turns into something else by magic are not errors in themselves.
Output: JSON with key ratings, a list of objects id, grammar, consistency, plot, category (S/P/N), reason (short specific reason). One record per ID, no duplicate IDs. Do not produce comparative summaries. The correctness decision will be computed automatically: S and all scores >=6.
